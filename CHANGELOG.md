# Changelog

## Unreleased

The full review behind these changes is in `docs/upgrade-scan-2026-10-31.md`.

### Changed
- Technical alerts use a freshness rule instead of the zero-delay rule, which no quote could meet because Yahoo's chart endpoint never states a delay (`exchangeDataDelayedBy` is absent from its responses). An alert now needs a positive price, at least 15 daily closes and a quote whose own timestamp is between one minute ahead (clock skew) and five minutes old; a source that states a delay above zero is still refused. During trading, a feed delayed by more than five minutes has quotes older than that, so the age check refuses them too, and from five minutes after a session's last trade until the next session trades no technical alerts fire (`src/utils/marketData.js`, mirrored in `native/modules/marketdata`).
- An alert is raised when its condition turns true, stays silent while the condition holds on later refreshes, and re-arms when it clears or a new exchange-local day starts; when several conditions turn true together, one alert is raised: the first of breakout, breakdown, bullish and bearish that the Settings → Alerts switches and Minimum score allow, so with Breakdown alerts off a bearish trend that turns true together with a breakdown still alerts. Every condition that turned true counts as raised, whether or not it was shown. Before, the de-duplication key included the quote time, so an unchanged condition raised a new alert on every refresh (`src/state/scanStore.js`, `src/utils/alertGates.js`).
- Settings → Alerts → **Minimum score** gates only Bullish alerts (2.1.0 applied it to every alert); breakout, breakdown and bearish alerts are raised whatever the score. The score rewards a dip and a bounce: a breakout, a fresh one-month closing high, has neither and scores 50 or less in practice, so the default minimum of 60 would drop every one, and breakdown and bearish warnings come from low-scoring setups. The Settings hint says so.
- A breakout is a price strictly above the highest daily close before the current session, with that high set at least nine sessions earlier, so a second top at the high resets the base. Before, the flag could only fire within 0.5 % below the old high. Breakdowns are unchanged and now pinned by tests. The messages describe the condition: "Price $X is above its prior one-month closing high." and "Price $X is no more than 0.5% above the lowest close since its one-month closing high" (amber alerts add how far below that high) (`src/utils/scoring.js`, `native/src/analysis.cpp`).
- The app's RSI is labelled "Simple RSI" wherever it appears (the Overview and Oil Watch columns, the stock page header, the score's "Simple RSI (14)" factor), because it is Cutler's simple 14-period RSI over one month of closes and differs from the Wilder RSI(14) that Finviz and TradingView show. The calculation is unchanged.
- Settings → Scanner → **Excluded sectors** apply only to broad "any sector" scans (the Overview's Any Sector scan and Discovery); a sector picked on the Overview is scanned in full. Before, picking an excluded sector paged through every screener page, showed "No stocks found matching your criteria." and reused that empty result. The setting's hint no longer names ETF rankings, which never read the list, and the Scanner section's description says what each desk takes from it.
- Ratings take their colour from the verdict on every desk: Strong Buy and Buy green, Hold amber, Sell and Strong Sell red; Strong Sell is no longer grey, and `getVerdict` returns the label only (`src/utils/scoring.js`). The Overview's Low scores tile is red. Small red text on a red wash or a selected row is lifted toward white to keep 4.5:1 contrast, and so is muted text on Discovery's selected row and the Overview's expanded row, and the instrument name on the Futures desk's selected row.
- `--text-faint` is raised so captions (quote and retrieval times, delay notes, table headers) reach 4.5:1 on the page and card grounds: `oklch(0.64 0.03 270)` in Pierce/27 and `#848e98` in Modern. It is no longer `--text-muted` × 0.73 (`src/styles/tokens.css`; `native/modules/palette/include/dip/palette.hpp` regenerated).
- Overview: the table renders the first 100 rows of the current sort and filter, with "Showing X of Y" always visible under it, a Show more button that adds up to 100 rows and Show all (N); sort, search and the rating filter still cover every scanned row. A Sector or Scan size pick starts its scan once it has been unchanged for 300 ms, and Refresh pressed meanwhile scans the pick the controls show. Columns have fixed widths, so the table keeps a minimum width of about 1,190 px and scrolls inside its panel in narrower windows (`src/pages/Dashboard.jsx`).
- All Overview scans share four screener-page and eight Yahoo-chart request slots, and the scope on screen is served first, requests already waiting included; a superseded run's waiting requests are never sent. A refresh keeps each earlier row, with its original score, quote time and retrieval time, until that row is rescored, and "Refreshing · previous scan retained…" shows while such rows are on screen.
- Desk auto-refresh pauses while the window is hidden (minimised or fully covered): a refresh that falls due then is skipped, and one catch-up refresh runs when the window is shown again. An Overview left open keeps scanning while hidden if any alert type is enabled, because its scans raise the alerts; its Market Pulse strip still pauses. The Futures desk keeps its own refresh-while-visible rule, without a catch-up. A new Overview sector or depth restarts its schedule, and so does opening another symbol on the stock page, so the symbol just opened is not fetched again moments later; its first refresh comes one full interval after it opens (`src/utils/useVisibleInterval.js`).
- Every desk requests Finviz's canonical URLs, which answer without a redirect: `https://finviz.com/screener/stocks?…` for the Overview and Discovery, `https://finviz.com/screener/etf?…` for ETF rankings, `https://finviz.com/stock?t=<symbol>&p=d` for the stock page, and `https://finviz.com/futures` for the Futures desk's page fallback (used when `api/futures_all` fails) and its **Verify on Finviz** link, instead of `futures.ashx`, which only redirects there (`src/services/futures.js`, mirrored in `native/modules/futures`). Discovery passes unusual volume as the signal `s=ta_unusualvolume`; as the filter it used before, Finviz ignored it, so the desk listed volatile stocks by volume whether or not their volume was unusual. A retried Finviz request names `https://finviz.com/screener/stocks` as its `Referer` instead of `screener.ashx` (`main.cjs`).
- Stock page: the 52-week strip is back. It reads Finviz's `52W High` and `52W Low` (Finviz no longer publishes `52W Range`), falls back to Yahoo's chart metadata, names its source in the caption and says "above the … 52-week high" or "below the … 52-week low" when the price is outside the range. The Key Statistics 52W tiles show the price's distance from Finviz's bounds. Finviz statistics, headlines and the research viewers load and fail independently of the Yahoo chart, and switching symbol never shows the previous stock's data. Headlines show the publisher Finviz names, with times labelled ET. The price axis spans the closes and both reference lines, with padding, never below $0 and with finer ticks below $1 (`src/pages/StockDetails.jsx`, `src/utils/finvizQuote.js`).
- Futures desk: an unchanged instrument (0.00 %, `-0` included) is flat, with no arrow, matching the advancing and declining counts. A sparkline is drawn only when its last sample is within max(0.5 % of the price, the session's high − low) of the quote; otherwise the desk says "Chart hidden: Finviz's samples end away from the quoted price, so they may not be recent." The focus chart shows Finviz's own time labels, and its caption names the sampling interval (5-minute, hourly, daily, weekly or monthly) instead of "intraday" (`src/services/futures.js`, `src/pages/Futures.jsx`).
- An unchanged quote or a zero average change is shown flat, not green, on the Overview (rows and the Avg Day Δ tile), in the Market Pulse strip and on Oil Watch, as on the Futures desk, and a stock at its one-month closing high reads 0.0%, not -0.0%.
- Oil Watch shows the 15 newest headlines, newest first and undated ones last, with high-impact matches lifted to the top; the publisher is the feed's `<source>`, and only the exact " - \<source>" suffix is removed from the title (`src/utils/oilNews.js`).
- Stored and imported settings must hold values the desk offers: refresh interval 0, 1, 5, 15 or 30 min; reuse window 0, 30, 90, 180 or 600 s; chart cache 0, 30, 60, 300 or 900 s; scan depth a whole number from 1 to 500 (2.1.0 accepted up to 10,000; v1 depths such as 20 still load as saved, and the Overview and the Settings desk list them as the selected option); minimum score 0–100, history limit 1–1000 and request timeout 1–600 s, all whole numbers; excluded sectors as distinct known sector ids. On load an invalid value falls back to its default and the excluded-sector list is repaired (unknown ids dropped, repeated ones kept once); an import reports every invalid value and applies nothing (`src/state/settingsSchema.js`).
- Seizure Mode is no longer in the header's theme list, where the 2.0.0 notes say the previous themes remain selectable. It starts from Settings → Appearance → **Run Seizure Mode…** after a photosensitivity warning. Its drone is silent when Settings → Alerts → Sound is off or the volume is zero, quieter below the default volume and never louder than before; Mute drone and End now are offered while it runs, and it still ends after 20 seconds and is never saved. When it ends, by End now or after the 20 seconds, focus on its controls returns to **Run Seizure Mode…** on Settings, or to the desk's heading elsewhere; in a narrow window its controls wrap upwards, clear of the alert history button.
- The window title names the desk ("Overview — Dip Analyzer PRO", "AAPL — Stock — Dip Analyzer PRO") instead of the fixed title noted under 2.0.0. Every desk has one `h1`. A desk you switch to opens at the top of the page instead of at the previous desk's scroll offset, and keyboard focus moves to its `h1`; leaving a failed desk, or recovering one in place, also puts focus on the heading (`src/App.jsx`, `src/components/ErrorBoundary.jsx`).
- The alert sound ships as a 21 KB MP3 loaded on first use (`src/assets/alien_ominous.mp3`), replacing the 353 KB WAV loaded at startup. To regenerate it, run `src/scripts/generate_audio.py`, which writes `src/assets/alien_ominous.wav`, found from the script's own location on any OS (it used a hard-coded Windows path), then run `ffmpeg -i alien_ominous.wav -codec:a libmp3lame -q:a 6 alien_ominous.mp3` in `src/assets`; ffmpeg 6.1.1 reproduces the shipped file byte for byte. `.gitignore` keeps the WAV out of the repository.
- Yahoo chart parsing builds its date formatters once per exchange zone instead of once per bar: on Node 22, `getYahooQuote` on a one-month chart went from about 3 ms to 0.2 ms and a 252-bar series from about 27 ms to 1 ms, with identical output. Quote and retrieval times likewise come from one formatter built on first use (`formatQuoteTime`, `src/utils/format.js`), so if the computer's time zone changes while the app runs, they keep showing the earlier zone, still labelled, until the window reloads or the app restarts.
- `formatCompact` picks its unit after rounding (999,950 prints 1.00M, not 1000.0K), and no formatter prints a negative zero in any locale: `formatPct(-0.004)` is `0.00%`, and a percentage carries `+` only when its rounded value is positive (`src/utils/format.js`, mirrored in `native/modules/format`).
- `npm run dev` uses `--strictPort`: if 127.0.0.1:5174 is taken it exits instead of moving to another port, which `npm run electron` (waiting on 5174) would miss.
- `build_exe.bat` builds with `npm run build:renderer` and `npm exec --no -- electron-builder --win nsis --x64 --publish never`, as CI does, and reports and lists `release\` (it said `dist\` and always printed "No .exe found"). It no longer deletes electron-builder's `winCodeSign` cache, is plain ASCII and is checked out with CRLF line endings (`.gitattributes`). It still asks for administrator rights.
- `.github/workflows/native-core.yml` runs on pushes to `master` only (the old feature branches are dropped), and pull requests that touch `tests/fixtures/**` or `.gitattributes` now trigger it too, besides those that touch the core or the code it mirrors; its Linux leg now really builds Release. `.github/workflows/windows-installer.yml` cancels only superseded pull-request runs, never a `master` run. Every workflow action is pinned by full SHA to its v7 release (checkout v7.0.1, setup-node v7.1.0, upload-artifact v7.0.2).
- ESLint applies its recommended rules to every `.js`, `.jsx`, `.mjs` and `.cjs` file, each with the globals of the place it runs: `main.cjs` as CommonJS on Node, `preload.cjs` as a sandboxed preload (the page's globals and `process`, none of Node's), the tests, scripts and configs as ES modules on Node; the React rules stay in `src/` (`eslint.config.js`). Before, `main.cjs`, `preload.cjs` and `scripts/*.mjs` got no rules at all, so a typo such as `isHtps` in `main.cjs` passed lint. `tests/lintConfig.test.js` checks the rules and globals each kind of file gets.
- eslint-plugin-react-hooks 7.0.1 → 7.1.1 (`^7.1.1`); the pages pass its rules.

### Fixed
- Settings → Data & sources → **Chart cache** and **Request timeout** were reset to Off and 12 s on every launch and import in 2.1.0; they now persist across restarts, imports and export → import round trips. The saved request timeout reaches the main process before the first desk renders, so the first provider requests after launch honour it.
- Minimum score, History limit and Request timeout applied on every keystroke, so clearing a field could set a 1 s timeout or cut the alert history. They now commit on blur or Enter; Escape, or an empty or non-numeric entry, restores the saved value, and out-of-range entries are clamped and rounded.
- Settings → Backup → **Copy** needs a clipboard write, which the desktop app denied along with every other permission; the app's own page may now write to the clipboard (see Security). When the write is refused, the export stays focused and fully selected with a "copy it manually" instruction, and a copy by hand is confirmed only when the whole export was selected.
- A burst of alerts showed one toast and played one sound per alert. At most three toasts are shown and the rest collapse into "+N more alerts — open history"; at most one sound plays per two seconds, and amber alerts share one audio context. The history keeps every raised alert up to its limit, and a malformed stored entry no longer blanks the window (`src/components/LiveAlerts.jsx`).
- Overview paging trusted the rows it accepted, so a row the parser skipped could end a scan early, and a page offset Finviz ignored repeated rows. Paging now ends at a short page, at the result count the page states or at a page of gainers; an ignored offset stops the scan with "Finviz returned rows from No. X for the page starting at No. Y."; look-ahead is at most four pages past the published ones; and a failed page past the end of the results no longer fails a complete scan.
- When a required numeric column (price and change; for the ETF views also the 1-month or 1-year performance or the dividend yield) cannot be read on any row, the scan stops with "Finviz screener schema changed: \<field> unparseable." and keeps the previous rows, marked stale. The parser binds columns by `data-column-id` first and by accessible name only for fields no id claims, tolerates extra columns, and reads typographic minus signs and no-break or thin spaces in numbers (`src/utils/finviz.js`).
- When every Yahoo chart request of a scan failed, the Overview showed unscored rows without a reason and reused the scan. It now says "Yahoo Finance scoring failed for all N names: \<reason> Technical scores are unavailable." and rescans on the next visit. After 16 chart failures in a row (two rounds of the eight chart slots) a scan stops requesting charts: with Yahoo unreachable, a depth-500 scan sends 23 chart requests instead of 500, still lists every Finviz row, scores names whose chart is cached and says "Yahoo Finance scoring failed for 16 names in a row: \<reason> Scoring stopped early, so technical scores are unavailable for N more names."
- The Overview's score breakdown gave the Yahoo quote time but not when that chart was retrieved, so on a screener row, whose Retrieved time is the Finviz page's, a score from a cached chart showed no retrieval time of its own. Its detail line now ends with "· retrieved \<time>"; a chart reused from the cache keeps its original retrieval time (`src/pages/Dashboard.jsx`).
- A render error anywhere blanked the whole window. The workspace, the routed desk and the alert layer now have error boundaries: a failing desk shows "This desk hit an error" with Reload and Go to Overview while the header and navigation keep working, an alert-layer failure shows "Alerts stopped after an error" with Reload, and unknown addresses show a Page not found desk (`src/components/ErrorBoundary.jsx`, `src/pages/NotFound.jsx`).
- A failed load of the stock page's chunk left stock links dead while the renderer re-rendered without end. The failure is no longer cached: if the idle warm-up fails, the first visit can still load the chunk (Chromium 156, which Electron 45 ships, fetches a failed module again), and when it cannot, the desk shows its error panel, whose Reload recovers once the network is back (`src/utils/preloadable.js`).
- Alien Fractal and Seizure Mode put a filter on `<body>`, which pulled the toasts, the alert history button and its drawer out of the viewport; their effects now run on fixed layers and follow Motion: Reduced.
- The header clipped its Theme select at narrow widths and high zoom; it now wraps, and no desk scrolls sideways at 320 px.
- The floating Alert history button could cover the focused control; every page now reserves its footprint (WCAG 2.4.11). Overview quote and retrieval times wrap instead of losing their time zone.
- TradingView charted share classes such as BRK-B as a spread (BRK minus B). They are charted as BRK.B, and a symbol TradingView would still read as a spread shows "TradingView has no chart for \<symbol>." instead of a wrong chart.
- If browser storage is denied or full, the Overview still opens; it starts on Any Sector and does not remember the pick.
- Stripping inline event handlers from provider HTML takes linear time, so a page with a long run of whitespace can no longer freeze the renderer (`src/utils/inertHtml.js`).
- Provider failures reach the desks in plain words, without Electron's "Error invoking remote method" prefix: "Finviz returned HTTP 429 Too Many Requests", Yahoo's own explanation ("Yahoo Finance returned HTTP 404 Not Found: No data found, symbol may be delisted"), "Could not reach Yahoo Finance (ENOTFOUND)" or "Request timed out after 12 s"; a Yahoo answer that is not chart data, such as a consent page served in its place, reads "Yahoo Finance did not return chart data." (a redirect to one is refused instead; see Security) (`main.cjs`, `src/utils/bridge.js`).
- `dip::jsonlite` read numbers with `strtod`, so under a comma-decimal C locale such as `de_DE.UTF-8` it read 6789.25 as 6789; it now uses `std::from_chars`. It also parses large objects in linear time, decodes provider bytes as WHATWG UTF-8 the way `Response.text()` does, and documents its 512-level nesting limit as a divergence (`JSON.parse` has none). `dip::marketdata` takes the host zone for local times from `TZ` as V8 does, and folds zone names as ASCII, so a Turkish `LC_CTYPE` no longer breaks `europe/istanbul`.
- Stopping `npm start` with SIGTERM, as an editor's Stop button or `kill` does, left Vite and Electron running, with Vite still holding 127.0.0.1:5174, so the next `npm start` failed with "Port 5174 is already in use". npm passes SIGTERM only to the shell it runs the script in, and that shell exited without passing it on to `concurrently`. `npm start` now runs `scripts/start.mjs` (with `scripts/launcher.mjs`), which starts Vite and then Electron itself, with no shell in between. It stops both on SIGINT, SIGTERM or SIGHUP, and also when the process that started it exits; a stop while Vite is still starting stops it at once. Quitting the app stops Vite, either one exiting stops the other, and it refuses to start while something already answers on 127.0.0.1:5174. `concurrently` is no longer a dependency.
- `npm run test:live` left its Vite dev server running on 127.0.0.1:5174 after every dev-mode run, because stopping it reached only `npx` (and on Windows its `cmd.exe`): the next `npm run dev` or `npm start` failed with "Port 5174 is already in use", and the next live run measured whatever answered there. It now runs Vite with Node itself (`node_modules/vite/bin/vite.js`, as CI does) and stops it when the run ends, even when writing `artifacts/live/report.json` fails; it refuses to start while something already answers on 127.0.0.1:5174, and fails at once, with Vite's own message, when its server exits before answering (`scripts/live-checks.mjs`, `scripts/verify-live-electron.mjs`).

### Security
- TradingView's loader script (`tv.js`) no longer runs in the app's document, which holds the desktop bridge: TradingView redeploys it, so it cannot be pinned, and whatever it ran there could call the bridge. The chart is TradingView's own embed page in an iframe on `https://s.tradingview.com` whose URL the app builds (`src/utils/tradingView.js`), sandboxed with `allow-scripts allow-same-origin` only (no popups, top navigation, downloads or forms) and sent without a referrer, so links inside the chart do nothing and its snapshot menu is hidden. The chart counts as loaded only after TradingView's ready message from its own origin; after 20 s without one, "TradingView chart did not load…" appears with a Retry chart button. The charts on both pages take their colours from the app theme.
- The production Content Security Policy (`vite.config.js`) is `default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'; img-src 'self'; font-src 'self'; media-src 'self'; connect-src 'self'; frame-src https://s.tradingview.com; worker-src 'self'; object-src 'none'; base-uri 'none'; form-action 'none'`. TradingView is gone from `script-src` and `connect-src`, the unused `data:`, `https:` and `blob:` sources are dropped, and inline styles stay allowed only because documents parsed from Finviz markup fall under the policy.
- Research webviews may only be attached to the stock page's five research sites over HTTPS on the default port: `finviz.com`, `www.chartmill.com`, `stockcharts.com`, `digital.fidelity.com` and `finance.yahoo.com` (2.1.0 accepted any HTTPS page). They run in an in-memory `research` session whatever partition a page asks for, so cookies and sign-ins made there, such as a Fidelity login, never reach the app's profile on disk and are gone when the app quits; that session denies every permission. Their media plays with sound only after a click or key press in that viewer (`autoplayPolicy: 'user-gesture-required'`), because Electron's default, `no-user-gesture-required`, lets an ad on a research page start playing sound by itself. Downloads keep Electron's save dialog, so the user confirms each file.
- A research viewer may open a link in the system browser only if it is HTTPS and comes within one second of a click, key press or tap in that viewer, one launch per gesture; scripted and ad popups are dropped silently. Before, any `window.open` from a research page or its ad frames reached the system browser without a click. The app's own links (news, StockFetcher, ETF Database) still open http(s) URLs in the browser.
- The navigation lock also covers server redirects (`will-redirect`), for the app window and the research viewers, and a `file://` URL naming another host, such as a Windows UNC share, is never taken for the app.
- `fetch-url` follows redirects itself and holds every hop to the same allowlist (HTTPS, an allowed host, the default port): a refused hop fails the request with "Refused redirect to \<origin>.", and a sixth redirect with "\<Provider> redirected more than 5 times." Cookies are kept in memory per host, each hop's under its own host, merged by name and deleted on `Max-Age<=0` or a past `Expires`; none is sent to another host. `fetch-yahoo-chart` checks the symbol, range and interval before any request exists and refuses every redirect with the same "Refused redirect to \<origin>.", never following or retrying it (Node's `fetch` used to follow a Yahoo redirect to any host); it retries server errors and network failures (never a 4xx answer) within the one request deadline, and reads an HTTP error answer from Yahoo for its explanation only up to 64 KB. Responses on both channels are read as streams and refused past 15 MB (`main.cjs`).
- Every permission request and check is still denied, with one exception: the app window's own page (its main frame, at the app's URL) may write text to the clipboard, which Settings → Backup → Copy needs. The TradingView frame and the research viewers get nothing.
- A server that asks for a TLS client certificate gets none: `main.cjs` answers Electron's `select-client-certificate` with no certificate for every page (the app window, its TradingView frame and the research viewers). Without that handler Electron 45 sends the first matching certificate from the system store without asking, so an ad frame in a research viewer could learn the name and organisation in a work, VPN or smart-card certificate. No provider or research site needs one; a site that requires one cannot be used in a viewer.
- Electron 45.0.0 → 45.2.1 (Node 24.22.1, Chromium 156.0.8078.75); the `package.json` floor is `^45.2.1`. Users get it only from a new release: the app has no auto-updater.
- electron-builder 26.8.1 → 26.18.1, still pinned exactly. This supersedes the 2.1.0 reason for the 26.8.1 pin: the `EPERM` on `win-unpacked.tmp` came from the directory rename in 26.15.3, the version the lockfile held then, and 26.18.1 retries that rename on `EPERM`, `EBUSY`, `ENOENT` and `EXDEV` before falling back to copy and delete. The old pin also kept GHSA-7g7r-gx96-252g: the AppRun launcher that app-builder-lib before 26.15.0 writes into an AppImage puts the launch directory on `LD_LIBRARY_PATH`, and that script runs on users' machines. `npm audit fix` then moved follow-redirects to 1.16.2, joi to 18.3.1 and zod to 4.6.7. `npm audit` went from 10 high and 16 moderate findings to 0 high and 20 moderate, all one advisory, GHSA-g3hv-75wg-m249 (minimatch before 11.0.1, a denial of service on crafted glob patterns), reached only through electron-builder's and ESLint's dependency trees, which run at build and lint time and are not packaged. This replaces the 2.1.0 note that the remaining moderate findings were in the build tool only. Do not run `npm audit fix --force`: its only fix is a downgrade to electron-builder 20.23.1.
- `.github/workflows/release.yml`: the workflow token is read-only and only the release job gets `contents: write`; the tag and commit SHA reach PowerShell through environment variables, never spliced into the scripts; `CSC_LINK` and `CSC_KEY_PASSWORD` are set only in the electron-builder step's environment, which does not isolate them: the job's earlier steps (npm install scripts, the tests, lint and the renderer build) run on the same runner and can still reach them and the write token; and the job uses no dependency cache. A `version` job checks the tag against `package.json` alongside `ci.yml` and `native-core.yml`, which `release.yml` now calls as reusable workflows (`workflow_call`) with the read-only token and no secrets; the release job waits for all three, so nothing is built or published until those checks pass on the tagged commit.
- `.gitignore` ignores `electron-builder.env`, `*.pfx` and `*.p12`: electron-builder loads `electron-builder.env` from the working directory by itself, so signing material must never be committed.

### Added
- `.github/workflows/ci.yml`: on every pull request and every push to `master`, a Linux job runs `npm ci`, `npm test`, lint, the production renderer build and the browser regressions against the Vite dev server and against `vite preview` of that build, with a read-only token and a 20-minute limit; a newer push cancels a pull request's running check. It runs `npm test` a second time with `TZ=America/New_York` and `LANG=de_DE.UTF-8`, after checking that Node resolved that zone and locale, since an `LC_ALL` would silently override `LANG`.
- `.github/workflows/native-core.yml`: an Ubuntu job builds the C++ core in Debug with AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer and runs the parity suites under them.
- `.github/dependabot.yml`: weekly npm and GitHub Actions updates. Electron's minor and patch updates come in a group of their own, so a security update never waits on tooling; other development-dependency minor and patch updates are grouped, and majors arrive as separate pull requests. electron-builder is left for a manual bump after `windows-installer.yml` has validated NSIS packaging.
- `npm test` now runs 319 tests. `main.cjs` and `preload.cjs` run in a `vm` harness against a stand-in for Electron (`tests/helpers/mainProcess.js`), so the window, navigation, webview, permission, client-certificate and network policies, the preload channel allowlist and the bridge contract are tested without an Electron binary. `tests/alertGates.test.js` checks the alert gates with real `analyzeStock` breakout and bullish series.
- `locale_regression` (CTest) reads JSON numbers and the recorded futures fixture under a comma-decimal C locale, and the marketdata parity suite repeats its zone-name cases under a Turkish `LC_CTYPE`; on Linux the configure step compiles `de_DE.UTF-8` and `tr_TR.UTF-8` into the build tree when glibc's locale sources are installed. Each check is skipped only where no such locale exists: once the configure step has compiled one, a locale the test cannot switch to fails `locale_regression` or the marketdata suite instead, and `locale_regression` says how to rebuild it.
